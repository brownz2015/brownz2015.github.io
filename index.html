About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Sociology at the University of Washington, Seattle, where I study social networks, political sociology, social movements, and social media. I expect to complete my degree in Spring 2027.

My work uses computational methods to understand political communication, online extremism and disinformation, and the ways social groups form, endure, and change.

Ongoing projects

Works in progress

01 · Dissertation

Post-January 6 QAnon: Network Resilience, Movement Identity, and Political Diffusion

Using Telegram forwarding data and computational text analysis, this project traces QAnon’s resilience after January 6 and the diffusion of its claims into American political discourse.

02 · Supported by the Minerva Research Initiative

Computational Kremlinology: Analyzing the Dynamics of Authoritarian Regime Elites Using Networks Constructed from Images and Text

Analyzes text- and image-derived networks to identify shifts in Russian elite career trajectories, including the relationship between media stance and later promotion or demotion.

03

3I/ATLAS Failed Prophecy: Network Contagion Analysis

Studies how claims that 3I/ATLAS was an incoming starship emerged, spread, and were abandoned or redirected across alien- and UFO-focused Reddit communities.

04

Contested Governance and Electoral Violence

Develops computational measures of how rebel groups, criminal gangs, and separatist organizations pursue institutional legitimacy and local governance.
